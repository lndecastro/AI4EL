Appendix - In Class Activities#

Activity 1 — Group Scenarios: AI for Email Communication#

In this activity, you will work in small groups on a real-world email scenario. Each group is assigned a different situation — one that mirrors the kinds of emails people face every day at work, at home, in their communities, and in schools.

Your job is to use the AI prompt templates from Section 1.2 of this class to handle your scenario as effectively as possible. At the end of the activity, each group will briefly present their scenario, what they tried, and what they learned.

Time: 25–30 minutes of group work, followed by a 20-minute class debrief.

What you will need:

  • Access to an AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or any assistant you have been using)

  • The Prompt Library templates from Section 1.2

  • A device to type and share your prompt and results

Tip

Instructions for every group

Before you start, make sure everyone has read the scenario. Then follow these steps:

  1. Agree on which template(s) from Section 1.2 you will use.

  2. Write your prompt together using the template as your starting point.

  3. Paste your prompt into an AI tool and read the result carefully.

  4. Refine your prompt at least once based on what you observe.

  5. Prepare a short 2–3 minute presentation for the class covering:

    • Your scenario in one sentence

    • The prompt you used

    • What the AI produced

    • What you changed in your second attempt

    • One thing AI did well, and one thing it missed

Scenario 1 — Workplace: Asking for What You Need#

Context

You work as an office administrator at a mid-size company. A few months ago, your family moved to a new home, and your commute is now nearly 90 minutes each way. It has been exhausting, and it is starting to affect your energy and focus at work. You would like to ask your manager if you could work from home two days a week.

You have never made a request like this before, and you are not sure how to word it without sounding like you are complaining — or making demands. You want to be professional, positive, and make the case that this arrangement can work for both you and your team.

Your task

Use AI to draft a professional email to your manager requesting a flexible work-from-home arrangement.

Template to use: Template 1 — Draft an email from bullet points

Use these bullet points as your starting content:

  • You have worked at the company for three years with a strong performance record

  • Your commute is now 90 minutes each way since your recent move

  • You are requesting to work from home on Tuesdays and Thursdays

  • You are open to a trial period and will remain fully available during regular work hours

  • You want to frame this as a benefit to both you and the company — fewer distractions, better focus, and continued strong output

Steps

  1. Fill in Template 1 with the details above. The recipient is your manager; the purpose is requesting a hybrid work arrangement; the tone is professional and positive.

  2. Run the prompt and read the result.

  3. Identify one thing you want to adjust — maybe the tone feels too stiff, or it is missing a personal touch.

  4. Use Template 2 or write a follow-up instruction to refine it.

Discussion questions

  • Did AI strike the right balance between personal and professional?

  • Did the email make a clear case without sounding like a complaint?

  • What would you change before actually sending this to a real manager?

Scenario 2 — Personal / Home: Responding Without Escalating#

Context

You have been renting your apartment for two years and have always paid rent on time. Last week, a pipe burst in the unit above yours, and water leaked through your bathroom ceiling. You reported it to building management immediately. A week passed with no repair and no update. You sent a polite follow-up email, and this morning you received the reply below.

You are frustrated — the ceiling is still damaged and you are worried about mold — but you know that responding in anger will not help. You need to write a reply that is calm, firm, and professional.

The email you received

From: Suncoast Property Management To: You Subject: RE: Bathroom Ceiling Water Damage — Unit 4B

Hi,

We are aware of the situation. Repairs take time and we have multiple units to manage across the building. There is no need to keep sending follow-up emails — you will be contacted when a technician becomes available.

The leak itself has stopped, so this is no longer an active emergency. We ask that you please be patient while we work through our maintenance queue.

Regards, Suncoast Property Management

Your task

Use AI to help you write a calm, professional reply that acknowledges their message, restates the urgency of the repair, and asks for a specific timeline — without creating unnecessary conflict.

Template to use: Template 3 — Respond to a difficult email

Important constraints to include in your prompt:

  • Do not sound angry or accusatory

  • Ask for a specific repair date or timeline

  • Mention the risk of mold or further damage as a factual concern, not a threat

  • Keep the tone respectful but firm

Steps

  1. Fill in Template 3 with the email above and your constraints.

  2. Read the result. Does it sound firm enough? Too aggressive? Too passive?

  3. Adjust your prompt to fine-tune the tone and run it again.

Discussion questions

  • How did AI handle the balance between being polite and being assertive?

  • Did it add anything helpful that you had not thought of?

  • Would you send this email as written? What, if anything, would you change?

Scenario 3 — Community / Volunteering: Turning a Rough Draft into a Real Email#

Context

You are helping coordinate a community food drive for a local nonprofit. You need to send an email to your neighborhood association email list to recruit volunteers for a one-day collection event next Saturday.

You wrote a quick draft this morning — but when you read it back, you realized it sounds a bit too casual, jumps around between ideas, and does not make it easy for people to know what to do or when to show up. You want to clean it up before you send it.

Your rough draft

Subject: Food Drive Volunteers Needed!!

Hey neighbors,

So we are doing a food drive next Saturday and we really need volunteers to help out because last year we were kind of short-staffed and it was a bit chaotic. Basically what we need is people to show up in the morning to help set up the collection tables at the community center and then also some people to help with sorting later. Also someone with a car to help with drop-offs would be great!

The whole thing runs from 8am to about 2pm but you do not have to stay the whole time, even an hour or two helps. We are collecting canned goods and non-perishables for the Harvest Hope food bank. They really need our help this time of year.

If you want to volunteer just reply to this email or text me. I think my number is in the directory but if not just message me here.

Thanks so much! Jordan

Your task

Use AI to improve this draft so it is more organized, professional, and easy to act on — while keeping Jordan’s warm, community-oriented tone.

Template to use: Template 2 — Improve an existing draft

Things to ask AI to improve:

  • Organize the information more clearly: what, when, where, and how to sign up

  • Make it easy for readers to know what volunteering involves and what to expect

  • Maintain the friendly, community spirit — just make it cleaner and clearer

Steps

  1. Paste the rough draft into Template 2 and describe what you want improved.

  2. Read the result. Is the community warmth still there? Is the key information easy to find?

  3. Ask AI to make one further adjustment — for example, writing a stronger subject line or adding a closing line that motivates people to sign up.

Discussion questions

  • What did AI keep from the original? What did it change?

  • Did it preserve Jordan’s personality, or did it lose something?

  • How would you use this kind of tool in your own life — do you ever send messages that could use this kind of cleanup?

Scenario 4 — Academic / School: Making Sense of a Long Thread#

Context

Your child’s school recently announced a change to its homework policy. A back-and-forth email thread has been going on between teachers, the assistant principal, and several parents for the past two days. You were copied on it, but the thread has grown long and you have not had time to read all of it carefully.

There is a parent meeting tonight at 6:30 PM. You have 15 minutes before you need to leave. You want to understand what is actually happening — who said what, what was decided, and what questions are still open — so you can participate meaningfully.

The email thread

Email 1 From: Mrs. Patricia Okafor (Assistant Principal) To: 4th Grade Parents; 4th Grade Teachers Subject: Updates to Homework Policy — Effective Next Month

Dear 4th Grade Families and Teachers,

Following our curriculum review, we will be implementing a new homework policy beginning the first week of next month. Under the new policy, daily homework will be limited to 20 minutes per night for 4th graders, replacing the current 45-minute guideline. Weekend homework will be eliminated entirely. Teachers will also shift toward project-based take-home assignments once per month rather than nightly worksheets.

We believe this change better reflects current research on student wellbeing and learning effectiveness at this age group. A formal letter will follow.

Regards, Mrs. Okafor

Email 2 From: Mr. David Reyes (4th Grade Teacher) To: Mrs. Patricia Okafor; 4th Grade Parents; 4th Grade Teachers Subject: RE: Updates to Homework Policy — Effective Next Month

Thank you for sharing this, Mrs. Okafor.

I want to flag a concern from our end. Several of my colleagues and I feel that 20 minutes may not be sufficient for students to practice key math and reading skills, particularly for students who are still catching up after last year. We support the elimination of weekend homework and the project-based model, but we would like to discuss the nightly time limit further before implementation.

Would it be possible to schedule a brief teacher meeting before the policy goes into effect?

Best, Mr. Reyes

Email 3 From: Sandra Whitmore (Parent) To: Mrs. Patricia Okafor; 4th Grade Parents; 4th Grade Teachers Subject: RE: Updates to Homework Policy — Effective Next Month

Hello everyone,

As a parent, I actually welcome this change. My daughter has been stressed about homework every evening and it has been affecting her sleep and her attitude toward school. I think 20 minutes is reasonable and I am glad weekends will be free.

That said, I do have a question: how will monthly project-based assignments work for families where both parents work full time? Some of these projects in the past have required a lot of parental involvement. Will there be support available at school for students who cannot get help at home?

Thank you, Sandra Whitmore

Email 4 From: Mrs. Patricia Okafor (Assistant Principal) To: 4th Grade Parents; 4th Grade Teachers Subject: RE: Updates to Homework Policy — Effective Next Month

Thank you both for the thoughtful responses.

Mr. Reyes — yes, we will schedule a teacher discussion before implementation. The policy will not go into effect until all teachers have had a chance to provide input. We want this to be a collaborative process.

Mrs. Whitmore — that is an excellent point. Projects will be designed to be completable independently, and we will offer an after-school homework help session on Thursdays for students who need additional support.

We look forward to continuing this conversation at tonight’s parent meeting.

Best, Mrs. Okafor

Your task

Use AI to summarize this thread so you can walk into tonight’s meeting fully prepared.

Template to use: Template 4 — Summarize an email thread

Steps

  1. Paste the full thread into Template 4 and run your prompt.

  2. Read the summary. Does it capture the main issue, each person’s position, and the open questions?

  3. Ask AI one follow-up question — for example: “Based on this thread, what is one good question I could ask at the meeting tonight?”

Discussion questions

  • Did the summary capture everything important? Did it miss anything?

  • How long did this take compared to reading the full thread yourself?

  • What follow-up question did AI suggest, and did you agree with it?

Scenario 5 — Workplace / HR: When the Email Is the Problem#

Context

You just received an email from your company’s Human Resources department about changes to your health insurance benefits. It is important — it affects your coverage starting next month — but you can barely make sense of it. It is full of insurance and legal terms that feel like they were written for a lawyer, not for you.

Before you can think about how to respond, you need to understand what this email is actually saying. Then you need to write back to HR to ask a simple, clear question.

The email you received

From: Benefits Administration, Human Resources To: All Employees Subject: Annual Open Enrollment — Benefit Plan Modifications and Action Required

Dear Employee,

Please be advised that as part of the organization’s annual open enrollment cycle, modifications to your current health benefit elections will take effect on the first day of the upcoming plan year. The transition from the current PPO structure to the newly introduced HDHP/HSA-compatible tiered plan requires all enrolled participants to either affirm their existing elections, modify their coverage tier, or elect a qualified alternative by the enrollment deadline.

Failure to submit an affirmative election or change request through the benefits portal by the stated deadline will result in automatic enrollment in the default plan option, which may differ materially from your current coverage in terms of deductible thresholds, co-insurance obligations, and in-network provider access.

Dependents currently enrolled under your plan are subject to the same re-enrollment requirements. Documentation confirming dependent eligibility may be required for continued enrollment under the new plan structure.

For participants currently utilizing a Flexible Spending Account (FSA), please note that FSA balances are not transferable to Health Savings Accounts (HSA) and that any remaining FSA balance will be subject to the plan’s use-it-or-lose-it provisions at year end.

If you have questions regarding the comparative benefits of available plan options, or require assistance navigating the portal, please contact the Benefits Administration team.

Regards, Benefits Administration Human Resources Department

Your task

This is a two-part challenge.

Part A — Translate the email

Use AI to convert this email into plain, easy-to-understand language so you know exactly what it is saying and what you need to do.

Template to use: Template 5 — Translate tone

In your prompt, ask AI to rewrite the email in plain English, as if explaining it to someone with no HR or insurance background.

Part B — Write a follow-up

Once you understand the email, use AI to draft a short reply to HR asking one clarifying question: whether your current family doctor is still covered under the new plan.

Template to use: Template 1 — Draft an email from bullet points

Steps

  1. Run Part A. Read the plain-language version — does it now make sense?

  2. Note: what was the most important thing buried in the original that the translation helped surface?

  3. Run Part B and draft your follow-up question to HR.

  4. Is the reply professional and concise?

Discussion questions

  • How often do you receive emails like this in real life — from HR, insurance companies, landlords, or service providers?

  • Did AI translation make you feel more confident or more uncertain about the content?

  • What are the risks of relying on AI to translate important documents you need to act on?
